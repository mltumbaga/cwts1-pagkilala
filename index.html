Part 01

Community Portrait

My ICT Community at San Mariano National High School MAIN

I consider the ICT community at San Mariano National High School MAIN meaningful because I learned technical skills there, served as ICT Club President, and used what I learned to help teachers and fellow students. The community gave me opportunities to lead, share knowledge, and contribute through practical ICT service.

01 · People and Groups

The community included ICT Club officers and members, fellow students, teachers, and the school's ICT educators and adviser. As ICT Club President, I helped lead the club and support technology-related learning.

02 · Places and Institutions

Important spaces included the school's computer laboratory and ICT learning areas. The school, its teachers, ICT instructors, and the ICT Club provided the setting and resources for learning computer systems, troubleshooting, and office applications.

03 · Relationships and Practices

The community connected through ICT Club activities, classroom learning, hands-on troubleshooting, and helping teachers with computer and printer problems. I also shared Microsoft Word, Excel, and PowerPoint skills with fellow students as they prepared for work immersion, and guided classmates and clubmates in computer repair and troubleshooting.

04 · Assets and Strengths

The community's strengths included students' willingness to learn, shared technical knowledge, the computer laboratory, guidance from ICT teachers and the club adviser, and opportunities to practice computer systems servicing. I contributed by helping repair teachers' computers and printers, teaching office tools, and guiding peers through troubleshooting.

05 · Community Concerns

One concern I can examine is that students may begin with different levels of confidence and experience in ICT. I should describe this as a community concern only if it matches what I personally observed; I should not assume that it affected every student or the whole school.

06 · My Contribution and Limitation

My contribution was sharing the ICT skills I had learned: helping teachers with computer and printer issues, guiding classmates and clubmates through troubleshooting, and teaching office applications for work immersion. A limitation to reflect on honestly is the boundary of my own knowledge as a student. I could help with tasks I understood, but I should identify a specific situation from my experience where I needed more guidance or could not immediately solve a problem before finalizing this point.

My Place in the Community

I was part of the ICT community at San Mariano National High School MAIN and served as ICT Club President. I helped guide clubmates and classmates in computer repair and troubleshooting, helped teachers with computer and printer issues, and taught fellow students office applications in preparation for work immersion. These experiences showed me how technical skills can support other people.

Part 02

Citizenship and Community Reflection

01 · My Understanding of Citizenship

For me, being a Filipino citizen is an honor and an opportunity to serve my country and my fellowmen. I consider it an honor to be Filipino, and I believe citizenship should encourage me to contribute to the people around me. Being part of a country means being part of a wider community, where our actions can affect others.

I believe citizenship involves more than identifying myself as Filipino. It also means recognizing my responsibilities and considering how I can help. I may not be able to solve every problem, but I can begin with the knowledge and skills I have and use them to assist people who need help.

02 · My Experiences as a Filipino

My experience as ICT Club President at San Mariano National High School MAIN shaped how I understand leadership and service. I learned that a true leader should be a role model to the members of the group. Leadership is not simply about having a title; it means leading by example and showing responsibility through actions.

I came to realize that my fellow students respected me not only because I was their leader, but because of what I achieved and how I treated everyone fairly. Leading them and helping them learn became one of the best experiences of my life as a person. It showed me that leadership can be meaningful when it helps other people grow.

03 · Rights and Responsibilities

I connect citizenship with respecting other people and using my abilities responsibly. In my ICT community, I tried to treat my classmates fairly and share the knowledge I had gained. These actions are small, but they reflect the kind of responsibility I believe people should practice in their communities.

I also believe technical knowledge should not be used to mislead or take advantage of others. People who learn a skill have a responsibility to consider how they use it. For me, being responsible means applying knowledge honestly and using it to help rather than harm people.

04 · Understanding My Community

The knowledge I gained through my ICT studies allowed me to help teachers and other students with computer-related tasks. By sharing what I learned, I also helped classmates and ICT Club members develop their own technical knowledge. This showed me that learning can benefit more than the person who first acquires the skill.

Some of my classmates and club members were able to use their skills to repair computers and printers outside school and sometimes earn extra allowance. This helped me see that technical education can have practical value beyond the classroom. Skills can support learning and create opportunities for students to apply what they know.

One challenge I experienced was that learners did not always adapt to new information at the same pace. Some needed more time to understand what I was teaching. I continued sharing my knowledge because I hoped they would eventually be able to perform technical tasks independently, even without me guiding them. This experience taught me that patience is important when helping others learn.

05 · My Goals and Responsibilities as an Iskolar ng Bayan

I enrolled in the Associate of Science in Information Technology program at UP Open University because I want to expand my academic knowledge and build on the technical skills I have already acquired. I know that I still have more to learn about technology and the IT industry, and I want to improve my understanding through further education.

I want the knowledge I gain to help me develop personally and become more capable of assisting people who need help. My ICT experience showed me that sharing skills can help students and teachers. As I continue studying, I hope to become better prepared to offer useful and responsible technical assistance.

For me, being an Iskolar ng Bayan should remind me to value education and consider how my knowledge can benefit others. I want to use what I learn responsibly and avoid using technical skills to mislead people.

06 · Pagtataya: Examining Myself

Reflecting on my experience helps me recognize both my strengths and my limitations. One strength I value is my willingness to share knowledge and help others learn. I learned that leadership is not just about being in charge; it is also about treating people fairly and helping them become capable of doing things on their own.

At the same time, I know I still need to improve my knowledge of information technology. This is one reason I chose to pursue ASIT. I do not want to assume that the skills I already have are enough for every situation. I want to keep learning, improve my understanding, and become more capable of helping others.

I also learned that teaching requires patience because people learn at different speeds. Recognizing this limitation reminds me to keep improving how I explain technical information and to understand that learning takes time.

07 · Conclusion: My Starting Point

My experience in the ICT community helped me connect citizenship with leadership, fairness, learning, and service. Being Filipino is an honor to me, and I see it as an opportunity to serve my country and fellowmen. My experience as ICT Club President taught me that leadership is not defined by a title alone but by the example a person sets and the way that person treats others.

I am glad that I was able to share knowledge that helped other students develop their skills and sometimes use them to help others. I recognize that I still have much to learn, which is why I want to continue my education and strengthen my technical abilities.

My starting point is a willingness to keep learning, lead by example, treat people fairly, and share knowledge responsibly. I hope to use what I learn not only to improve myself but also to help people who may benefit from my skills.
